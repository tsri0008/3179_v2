Australian multicultural communities

Many Communities, One Australia

Almost three in ten people living in Australia were born in another country. This page follows how that happened: who we are, where people came from, and how the mix is changing.

Data sources and credits

The charts use three statistical datasets (two from the ABS and one from the Department of Home Affairs) and three geographic files. Several charts combine two sources: chart 5 joins ABS population estimates to country centre points, chart 6 joins Home Affairs visa data to country centre points, and chart 7 joins Census language data to state boundaries.

  • Australian Bureau of Statistics. (2022). Cultural diversity: Census, 2021 [Data set]. https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/people/people-and-communities/cultural-diversity-census/2021
  • Australian Bureau of Statistics. (2026). Australia’s population by country of birth, June 2025 [Data set]. https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/people/population/australias-population-country-birth/latest-release
  • Department of Home Affairs. (2025). Historical migration statistics, 2024–25 (Tables 1.1, 1.2, 3.1 and 3.3) [Data set]. https://data.gov.au/data/dataset/historical-migration-statistics
  • Natural Earth. (n.d.). Admin 0 – Countries, 1:110m [Data set]. https://www.naturalearthdata.com
  • rowanhogan. (n.d.). australian-states [Data set]. GitHub. https://github.com/rowanhogan/australian-states
  • mledoze. (n.d.). countries [Data set]. GitHub. https://github.com/mledoze/countries

Data were filtered, regrouped and summed from the original files without changing any reported values; the cleaned files and notes are in the data folder. Charts are built with Vega-Lite. Typefaces: Fraunces and Source Sans 3 (Google Fonts).

AI use: Claude (Anthropic) was used to help clean the data, and select colour pallete. I checked the numbers against the source files and edited the text.
